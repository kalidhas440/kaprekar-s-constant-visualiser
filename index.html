What is Kaprekar's Constant?

Discovered by Indian mathematician D.R. Kaprekar in 1949, the number 6174 holds a mesmerising property.

🔢

The Routine

Take any 4-digit number with at least two different digits. Arrange its digits in descending and ascending order to form two numbers. Subtract the smaller from the larger. Repeat with the result.

🌀

The Convergence

No matter which valid 4-digit number you start with, this process will always reach 6174 within at most 7 iterations. Once you hit 6174, the routine loops forever: 7641 − 1467 = 6174.

💡

Why It Matters

Kaprekar's constant is a beautiful example of a mathematical fixed point. It reveals hidden structure in our number system and has fascinated recreational mathematicians for over 70 years.

How It Works

A worked example using the number 3524.

①

Start: 3524

Sort descending → 5432
Sort ascending → 2345
5432 − 2345 = 3087

②

Step 2: 3087

8730 − 0378 = 8352

③

Step 3: 8352

8532 − 2358 = 6174

✓

Converged!

We reached 6174 in just 3 steps. The process now loops forever.
